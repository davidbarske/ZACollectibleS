The Edge, Relief and Surface Often Say More Than the Portrait.

A useful first review does not begin with a catalogue price. It begins with the physical evidence: diameter, weight, metal, die alignment, edge treatment, strike, wear and any signs of cleaning or tooling.

  • Attribution Ruler, mint, denomination, type and reference.
  • Condition Wear, deposits, corrosion, cleaning and damage.
  • Consistency Whether style, fabric and manufacture agree.
  • Context Provenance, old tickets, prior grading and comparable sales.

One Group, Several Research Paths.

A mixed group can contain ancient bronze, modern gold and commemorative material. Treating it as one undifferentiated lot hides the evidence. The useful route is to separate by period and metal, photograph both faces and edges, map prior labels and only then decide what needs deeper research or market testing.
