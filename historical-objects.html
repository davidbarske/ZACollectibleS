The Label, Letter or Photograph May Be Part of the Object’s Meaning.

Historical material rarely arrives in a clean category. A tool, badge, letter, map or souvenir can require material analysis, archival research and family knowledge before its significance becomes clear.

  • Function What the object was made to do and for whom.
  • Material Construction, wear, repair and signs of age.
  • Association Names, places, events and linked documents.
  • Chain of custody How the object moved between people and collections.
Archive example

Do Not Tidy Away the Evidence.

Boxes, envelopes, handwritten notes and old photographs can look peripheral, yet they often preserve names, dates and relationships that cannot be recovered once separated. The first useful act is careful recording, followed by a research map that distinguishes observation, family account and documentary proof.

  1. 01 Photograph the group as found
  2. 02 Map names, dates and associations
  3. 03 Catalogue and present the linked story
