Investor-stage legal architecture

This document demonstrates the intended depth and risk architecture of the operating site. South African counsel must review entity details, service-specific schedules and statutory notices before commercial launch.

1. Acceptance, Scope and Contract Formation

By accessing this website, submitting material, requesting services, registering an account or participating in a sale, the user agrees to these general conditions together with any service schedule, engagement letter, catalogue condition, privacy notice, quotation or transaction-specific agreement expressly incorporated by reference. If a transaction-specific agreement conflicts with these general conditions, the transaction-specific agreement prevails only to the extent of that conflict.

Website content, automated acknowledgements, provisional opinions and invitations to enquire do not constitute an offer capable of acceptance unless ZA Collectibles identifies the relevant item, service, consideration and material terms in writing. No silence, delay, website interaction or transmission of photographs creates a mandate, agency, fiduciary duty, custody arrangement or obligation to buy, sell, value or research an item.

2. Definitions and Interpretation

“Client” means the person identified in an accepted engagement. “User” means any website visitor or account holder. “Item” includes an object, group, collection, document, certificate, image, digital record or associated material. “Opinion” includes any preliminary identification, condition observation, authenticity comment, estimate, appraisal, valuation range, market view or recommendation. “Remote Review” means work performed without physical custody or inspection. “Services” includes appraisal, research, authentication support, acquisition, sale, consignment, auction, collection management, presentation, preservation support and digital collection intelligence.

Headings aid navigation and do not limit meaning. “Including” means including without limitation. A reference to law includes amendments and successor legislation. Singular includes plural where context permits. Electronic writing and data messages may satisfy a writing requirement to the extent permitted by applicable law.

3. Website Information, Demonstration Content and No Reliance

General editorial content explains methods and collecting concepts. It is not an individual appraisal, legal opinion, tax opinion, conservation instruction or investment recommendation. Investor-stage mock records, auction results, team profiles, interfaces and case studies may demonstrate intended future operation and must not be treated as completed client work unless expressly marked as verified.

The user must not rely on screen colour, image scale, automated translation or display-device rendering as a precise representation of material, tone, dimensions or condition. Historical market information does not guarantee future demand, liquidity or price.

4. Enquiries, Instructions and Client Responsibilities

The client must provide lawful, accurate and sufficiently complete information, disclose known repairs, treatments, disputes, claims, restrictions and third-party interests and identify the decision the requested work must support. ZA Collectibles may pause, decline or re-scope work when instructions remain ambiguous, evidence conflicts, identity cannot be verified or the requested purpose creates an unacceptable legal, ethical or technical risk.

The client remains responsible for retaining originals, making safe backups, protecting fragile items and obtaining independent advice where a decision has legal, tax, insurance, fiduciary or conservation consequences.

5. Remote Reviews and Physical Appraisals

A Remote Review can support probable identification, visible condition observations, evidence-gap analysis and provisional market context. It cannot exclude concealed repairs, treatments, substitutions, internal defects, altered marks, sophisticated reproductions or material differences invisible in the supplied media. Grade, authenticity and variety may depend on calibrated measurement, specialist equipment, laboratory testing, physical handling or comparison with authoritative reference material.

An Opinion states its scope, evidence basis, effective date, purpose and material limitations. Unless expressly agreed, it does not constitute a warranty, guarantee, certificate of authenticity or promise of a sale result. ZA Collectibles may revise an Opinion when better evidence becomes available.

6. Valuation Basis, Date and Purpose

Collector-market value, auction estimate, dealer-purchase value, orderly realisation, forced-sale value, insurance replacement and estate reporting answer different questions. The client may use a valuation only for its stated purpose, jurisdiction, assumptions and effective date. Values may change because of attribution, condition, provenance, supply, fashion, currency, regulation, platform policy, buyer depth and transaction costs.

Unless stated otherwise, figures exclude taxes, buyer’s premiums, seller’s commissions, shipping, restoration, certification, insurance, customs, exchange losses and third-party fees. No valuation is an assurance that a buyer exists at the stated level.

7. Authentication, Certificates and External Specialists

Third-party certificates, labels, holders and database entries are evidence, not substitutes for checking whether the document relates to the submitted item and remains current. ZA Collectibles may rely reasonably on external laboratories, databases and specialists but does not assume responsibility for their independent acts, omissions, turnaround times or changing standards.

Where material risk remains, ZA Collectibles may recommend escalation, quarantine a conclusion, refuse a definitive attribution or describe competing hypotheses. That restraint forms part of the service.

8. Buying, Private Sale and Consignment

No item is accepted for purchase or consignment until ownership, authority, identity, condition, reserve, commission, expenses, withdrawal rights, custody, insurance, payment and delivery terms are recorded in writing. ZA Collectibles may recommend direct purchase, private treaty, public auction or referral to another specialist route.

The consignor warrants lawful title, full authority to sell and disclosure of all liens, co-ownership, export restrictions, cultural-property issues, estate interests and authenticity disputes. The consignor indemnifies ZA Collectibles against loss arising from a material breach of those warranties, subject to applicable law.

9. Auctions and Curated Sales

Each auction may have catalogue-specific conditions covering registration, bids, reserves, increments, extensions, buyer’s premium, taxes, payment, collection, shipping, default and post-sale remedies. Estimates guide bidding and do not predict the hammer price. ZA Collectibles may combine, divide, withdraw, re-offer or decline lots where permitted by the applicable sale conditions.

A bid is binding when accepted by the auction mechanism. ZA Collectibles may reject suspicious or technically compromised activity, require identity and source-of-funds information and correct clerical catalogue errors before settlement where law permits.

10. Title, Illicit Trade, Restricted Materials and Cultural Property

ZA Collectibles may refuse any item reasonably suspected to be stolen, unlawfully exported, looted, fraudulently altered, subject to sanctions, derived from protected species or regulated under weapons, heritage, archaeological, precious-metals or anti-money-laundering rules. The user authorises reasonable provenance, identity and database checks proportionate to the risk.

Possession does not itself prove title. The client must disclose estate, family, partnership, trust, marital, creditor and insurance interests that may affect authority to instruct or dispose of an item.

11. Photographs, Digital Records and Client Portal

The client grants ZA Collectibles a non-exclusive licence to copy, crop, convert, annotate and securely store submitted media for the accepted service, internal quality control, record continuity and legal compliance. Public marketing use requires separate permission unless images have been supplied expressly for publication.

Prototype artificial-intelligence features provide decision support only. Automated outputs may be incomplete or wrong and must not be treated as conclusive identification, authentication, grading, valuation or legal advice. Account credentials remain the user’s responsibility. ZA Collectibles may suspend access to protect data, systems or other users.

12. Personal Information and Electronic Communications

Personal information will be processed for legitimate service delivery, identity verification, fraud prevention, recordkeeping, communication and legal compliance in accordance with the Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 and the applicable privacy notice. Electronic communications and transactions are subject to the Electronic Communications and Transactions Act 25 of 2002 where applicable.

The user must not transmit unnecessary identity documents, financial credentials, health information or third-party personal data. No internet transmission offers absolute security. ZA Collectibles will apply proportionate organisational and technical controls and will retain records only for legitimate operational, legal and evidential periods.

13. Intellectual Property and Permitted Use

ZA Collectibles retains rights in original reports, taxonomies, presentation systems, interface designs, photographs, editorial work and collection architectures, excluding pre-existing client material and credited third-party content. The client may use a paid report for its stated internal purpose but may not alter it materially, remove limitations, represent it as a different-purpose certificate or resell it as a standalone publication without written permission.

Open-access and public-domain source material remains subject to its source metadata, credit conventions and any non-copyright restrictions identified by the originating institution.

14. Risk Allocation and Limitation of Liability

To the maximum extent permitted by law, ZA Collectibles is not liable for indirect, consequential or speculative loss, loss of opportunity, market movement, emotional value, third-party platform failure or decisions made outside the stated scope of an Opinion. Nothing excludes liability that cannot lawfully be excluded, including liability arising from fraud, wilful misconduct or any mandatory consumer protection.

The client must notify ZA Collectibles promptly of an alleged error and allow a reasonable opportunity to inspect the relevant evidence and mitigate loss. Any monetary cap, exclusion or indemnity remains subject to the Consumer Protection Act 68 of 2008 where applicable and to the transaction-specific agreement.

15. Fees, Commissions, Premiums and Third-Party Expenses

Fees may comprise fixed professional charges, hourly charges, minimum fees, valuation scales, seller’s commission, buyer’s premium, photography or catalogue charges, storage, insurance, transport, laboratory fees, specialist fees, platform charges and agreed success-linked components. The applicable quotation or sale conditions must identify the charging basis with sufficient clarity for the client to understand the likely total cost before commitment.

Third-party expenses reasonably incurred within an approved mandate remain payable whether or not the research produces a definitive attribution, a laboratory confirms the proposed identity or an item sells. ZA Collectibles must obtain further approval before materially exceeding an agreed expense limit, except where immediate expenditure is reasonably necessary to protect an item or comply with law.

16. Payment, Set-Off, Default and Tax

Invoices are payable in the stated currency, by the stated date and without deduction or set-off except where law requires otherwise. A payer must use the reference supplied and remains responsible for bank charges, exchange differences and payment-provider fees. Ownership of reports, presentation materials or purchased items may remain with ZA Collectibles until cleared funds and all associated charges have been received.

Where payment is overdue, ZA Collectibles may suspend work, withhold delivery, retain possession where lawfully entitled, cancel an unperformed mandate or charge lawful interest and recovery costs after notice. No default remedy permits unfair conduct or removes a mandatory consumer right. VAT or other tax will be charged where applicable and identified on the relevant invoice.

17. Custody, Condition, Insurance and Risk of Loss

No item enters ZA Collectibles’ custody until an authorised representative accepts it and records a receipt, collection reference or other written acknowledgement. The client must disclose unusual fragility, contamination, hazardous material, active deterioration, weapon status, high value or handling restrictions before delivery. ZA Collectibles may refuse unsafe packaging or require specialist transport.

A condition note records visible condition for custody control and does not constitute a full conservation report unless expressly commissioned. Risk, insurance responsibility and the standard of care applicable during custody must follow the engagement or sale schedule. Sentimental, consequential and undocumented values may not be insurable. The client must maintain appropriate cover until written confirmation that responsibility has transferred.

18. Collection, Shipping, Export and Import

Collection and delivery dates remain provisional until payment, identity checks, packing requirements and any legal restrictions have been satisfied. The party arranging carriage bears the allocation of risk specified in the transaction terms. A courier’s acceptance does not prove that packaging, insurance or route is suitable for a fragile, valuable, regulated or culturally sensitive item.

The buyer, seller or client remains responsible for import permits, export permits, customs declarations, duties, endangered-species documentation, weapons compliance, heritage restrictions and destination-country rules unless ZA Collectibles expressly accepts a defined part of that work in writing. A delay, seizure or refusal by an authority does not automatically cancel an otherwise binding transaction.

19. Withdrawal, Cancellation and Change of Instructions

A client may request cancellation or a change of scope in writing. ZA Collectibles may charge for work completed, commitments made, third-party costs incurred, catalogue or marketing work already undertaken and any contractual withdrawal fee that remains lawful and proportionate. An auction consignor’s ability to withdraw a lot may narrow after cataloguing, publication or the opening of bidding.

ZA Collectibles may withdraw from an engagement where information is materially false, title is disputed, payment is overdue, the item presents an undisclosed risk, the requested conduct is unlawful or the professional relationship no longer permits reliable work. Where practical, ZA Collectibles will provide notice and a reasonable route for return or transfer of the material.

20. Returns, Cooling-Off Rights and Post-Sale Remedies

Any statutory cooling-off, cancellation, repair, replacement, refund or other remedy applies according to the nature of the transaction, the status of the parties and the governing legislation. Auction sales, private treaties, remote transactions, personalised services and goods purchased for a particular disclosed purpose may attract different rules. No website statement should be read as removing a non-excludable right.

A buyer raising a material catalogue error must preserve the item, packaging, labels and transaction records and must not clean, repair, alter, mount, dismantle or submit the item for destructive testing without written agreement. ZA Collectibles may require inspection and reasonable evidence before deciding whether rescission, price adjustment, correction or another remedy is appropriate.

21. Agency, Bidding, Conflicts and Related Parties

ZA Collectibles may act as principal, agent for a seller, adviser to a buyer or market intermediary depending on the transaction. The relevant role must be identified where it materially affects duties, remuneration or information rights. No general website description creates a fiduciary relationship beyond the accepted mandate.

Potential conflicts may arise from ownership, financial interest, prior work, referral arrangements, specialist relationships, bids placed on behalf of clients or competing instructions. ZA Collectibles will disclose and manage a material conflict where required and may decline one or more instructions if informed consent and practical safeguards cannot protect the integrity of the process.

22. Record Retention, Evidence Integrity and Destruction

ZA Collectibles may retain engagement records, invoices, identity checks, object images, catalogue descriptions, condition notes, research sources, correspondence and versioned opinions for periods reasonably required by operational, evidential, tax, fraud-prevention, insurance and legal obligations. Retention periods may differ by record type and may continue after an account closes.

Original client documents should not be destroyed without authority. Digital records may be migrated, compressed or converted while preserving material content. Where deletion is lawful and appropriate, ZA Collectibles may retain a minimal suppression, transaction or dispute record necessary to demonstrate compliance or prevent the same data from being reintroduced inadvertently.

23. Website, Portal and Acceptable Use

A user must not probe security, bypass access controls, scrape protected records, upload malicious code, impersonate another person, interfere with bidding, manipulate prices, submit unlawful imagery or use automated systems in a manner that degrades the service or extracts a substantial part of the site. Public indexing of ordinary editorial pages does not authorise bulk reproduction or commercial dataset creation.

ZA Collectibles may rate-limit, block, suspend or preserve evidence of activity reasonably associated with fraud, abuse, intrusion, rights violations or operational harm. The user must notify ZA Collectibles promptly if credentials, confidential links or client records may have been compromised.

24. Force Majeure, Platform Failure and Business Continuity

Neither party is responsible for delay caused by circumstances beyond reasonable control, including severe weather, fire, civil disorder, transport interruption, power failure, telecommunications failure, cyber incident, labour disruption, authority action, border restriction, epidemic or failure of a material third-party platform. The affected party must take reasonable steps to mitigate disruption and resume performance.

Timed bidding, payment and portal functions depend on networks and external infrastructure. Where a material technical failure affects fairness or record integrity, ZA Collectibles may pause, extend, reopen, cancel or reconstruct the affected process in accordance with the specific sale conditions and available audit evidence.

25. Complaints, Mediation, Governing Law and Jurisdiction

A complaint should identify the engagement, item, disputed act, supporting evidence and proposed resolution. The parties should first attempt good-faith written resolution, then mediation where proportionate. Urgent protective relief remains available notwithstanding that process.

These conditions are governed by South African law. Subject to any mandatory consumer forum or jurisdiction, the parties consent to the jurisdiction of the courts of the Western Cape. If a provision is invalid or unenforceable, it is severed or read down to the minimum extent necessary without invalidating the remainder.
