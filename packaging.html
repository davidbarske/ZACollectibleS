Before & After

Four Collection Problems. Four Different Interventions.

The work begins with the actual failure: a coin box with no order, mixed heirlooms without relationships, loose gemstones without safe comparison or research separated from the objects it explains. Presentation earns its value by correcting that failure.

Before · Loose boxAfter · Archival cabinet
Case Study 01 · The Box of Coins

Loose Coins Become an Identifiable Cabinet

Problem: dozens of coins mixed in a reused box, with abrasion risk, no sequence and several handwritten notes detached from their objects.

Intervention: non-destructive sorting, provisional attribution, duplicate mapping, inert holders, numbered trays and a photographic inventory.

Research deliverable: a custom collection booklet records each type, unresolved questions, missing dates and the evidence needed for higher-value attribution.

Result: the owner can see what exists, what repeats, what deserves specialist attention and which gaps could turn accumulation into a coherent collection.

Before · Mixed contentsAfter · One collection
Case Study 02 · The Box of Different Things

Five Object Types Recover Their Shared Story

Problem: medals, coins, a watch, stamps, jewellery and family papers stored together but understood separately.

Intervention: material triage, acid-free sleeves, fitted compartments, linked identity cards and a family-provenance chronology.

Presentation deliverable: a modular case keeps unlike materials safely separated while the catalogue and numbering system preserve their relationship.

Result: a collection that can be viewed, insured, researched and passed forward without dissolving into unrelated objects.

Before · Loose specimensAfter · Acrylic reference set
Case Study 03 · Gemstones in Acrylic Boxes

Loose Specimens Become a Comparable Reference Set

Problem: gemstones and mineral specimens stored in pouches with provisional notes, inconsistent protection and no stable identifier.

Intervention: individual acrylic boxes, removable identification slips, collection numbers, scale photography and certificate cross-references.

Research deliverable: a register separates observed characteristics from reported origin or treatment and identifies which specimens justify laboratory escalation.

Result: safer handling, immediate comparison and a system that can absorb revised identifications without rebuilding the collection.

Before · Evidence separatedAfter · Object and dossier linked
Case Study 04 · Research-Led Display

The Mount, Booklet and Digital Record Work Together

Problem: small objects were visually attractive but detached from labels, certificates, measurements and the research that made them intelligible.

Intervention: custom recesses, conservation-safe supports and removable resin or epoxy-set elements only where material compatibility and reversibility permit.

Research deliverable: a designed booklet combines object photography, attribution, provenance transcription, references, uncertainty notes and future research tasks.

Result: the object, evidence and collection narrative remain connected in physical and digital form while the display remains clean.

Presentation studio

Not Packaging for Packaging’s Sake.

The aim is to add context, consistency and durability. A good presentation system carries useful evidence with the object and creates a coherent visual language across the collection.

Reference Cards

Compact, consistent cards pairing the object with identification, period, context and useful evidence.

Booklets & Dossiers

Longer-form histories, catalogues, research notes and collection narratives.

Display Systems

Cases, holders and presentation structures designed around the collection rather than generic retail packaging.

Digital Records

Structured images and metadata that can travel with the collection and support future re-assessment.
